What Often Gives a Deepfake Video Away

Common tells in synthetic/manipulated video -- treat these as a caution, not a guarantee:

  • Blinking that's too rare, too regular, or missing entirely.
  • Lighting or shadows on the face that don't match the rest of the scene.
  • Blurring, warping, or flickering around the edges of the face, hairline, or ears.
  • Lip movements slightly out of sync with the audio.
  • Unnatural or absent head/body movement while the face keeps changing expression.

As these tools improve, some fakes will look completely convincing. That's exactly why the rule is PAUSE, THINK, REPORT -- not "look closely enough."

Seeing is not proof of identity.

PAUSE. Do not let urgency control the decision.

THINK. Does the request make sense?

REPORT. If the request is suspicious, use the Phish Alert button or the appropriate trusted reporting channel.
